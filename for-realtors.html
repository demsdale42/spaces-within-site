For Realtors

You’ve staged what’s in the home. Has anyone looked at what’s of it?

For agents who want every listing to feel as good as it looks.

The photos are sharp. The rooms are staged. The price is right. And now and then a buyer walks through a home that has everything and says only, “I don’t know. It just doesn’t feel right.”

Some of what a home feels like comes from what’s of it, not what’s in it.

Staging, repairs and pricing all work on what’s in the home. A home assessment looks at the part no one can stage.

The Difference

In the home, and of the home.

What’s in a home is what you can change before the first showing: paint, furniture, lighting, landscaping. It’s the work you already do well.

What’s of a home is what you can’t see and can’t stage. What runs beneath the foundation, and what runs through the walls. An earth energy line crossing a bedroom. An electrical panel, a router or a run of wiring sitting right where a buyer will sleep or work.

BioGeometry® is the science of the quality of energy in a space. Bobby reads three layers in a home: what’s beneath it, what’s within it, and you, meaning how a body responds. He places small solutions where the reading calls for them, then re-measures before he leaves.

A home can be finished, staged and photographed, and still never have been looked at this way.

What It Is, and Isn’t

Clear about what this is, and what it isn’t.

A home assessment is not a home inspection and doesn’t replace one. It makes no statement about a property’s condition, value or marketability, and Bobby doesn’t promise that a home will sell faster or for more. Questions of disclosure stay between you, your client and your broker.

What it does is read the space itself, and place small pieces of clear acrylic in the least noticeable spots in each room, so the home stays just as it was staged.
