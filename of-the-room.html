For Well-Designed Homes

Not what’s in the room. What’s of the room.

For homes that have already been done well.

You’ve put real time and care into your home. The bedroom is the one you’d happily have photographed. The home office is as efficient as it is handsome. Every choice was deliberate.

A home like that should feel like a sanctuary. Does it?

When the answer is “almost,” another piece of furniture is rarely what closes the gap. The cause is usually something more foundational.

The Difference

In the room, and of the room.

What’s in a room is what you chose: the furniture, the lighting, the art, the devices on the desk. A good designer can get every bit of it right.

What’s of a room is what you didn’t choose and can’t see. What runs beneath the house, and what runs through its walls. An earth energy line crossing a bed. An electrical panel, a router or a run of wiring sitting right where you sleep or work.

BioGeometry® is the science of the quality of energy in a space. Bobby reads three layers in a home: what’s beneath it, what’s within it, and you, meaning how your body responds. He places small solutions where the reading calls for them, then re-measures before he leaves.

You can finish every room and still never have looked at this one.

Does This Sound Like Your Home?

A few questions worth asking.

  • The bedroom is perfect on paper. Does it feel like a sanctuary when you walk in?
  • Do you get more done at a corner table in a coffee shop than at the home office you built for yourself?
  • Is deep, restful sleep still waiting on one more change: a different mattress, new linens, the bed on the other wall?

If you’ve answered everything you can see, the question left is the room itself.

What an Assessment Is
